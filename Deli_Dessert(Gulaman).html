DESSERT

GULAMAN

What is Gulaman?

Gulaman is a Filipino term for agar-agar, a type of gelatin made from dried seaweed. It is commonly used in various desserts and beverages in the Philippines. It is a popular ingredient in Filipino cuisine, and its texture is similar to gelatin. Gulaman can be prepared in various flavors, such as pandan, strawberry, mango, and ube.

What made Gulaman famous?

- Gulaman became famous in the Philippines primarily due to its versatility and suitability for creating a wide variety of desserts and beverages.

    INGREDIENTS

  • 2 pcs. fresh coconuts, cut in half and scrape (yield 3¼ cups), reserved ½ cup
  • 2½ cups sago, cooked
  • 3 cups pandan gulaman, cooked, reseved ½ cup
  • 1 (370ml) alaska crema-asada, chilled
  • 1 (370ml) evaporated milk
  • 1 (300ml) condensed milk
  • ½ cup coconut water or water
  • ½ cup pandan gulaman
  • ½ cup fresh coconut
  • Whipped cream (optional)

STEPS:

  1. Dissolve the powdered gulaman in cold water or another liquid, following the ratio specified on the package. Use less liquid for a firmer texture.
  2. Bring the mixture to a boil over medium-high heat, stirring constantly until fully dissolved. You can add flavor enhancers like pandan leaves or vanilla extract if using unflavored gulaman. Add sugar, if necessary.
  3. Strain the mixture into a mold to remove any undissolved particles.
  4. Allow it to set at room temperature or in the refrigerator before cutting into pieces and serving.
  5. Tear or cut the bar into smaller pieces so they dissolve faster.
  6. Add enough water or another liquid to submerge the gulaman. Refer to the package instructions for the specific amount of water recommended.
  7. Soak the gulaman for about 30 minutes to rehydrate and soften it. Note that some brands may not require pre-soaking; check the packaging for specific instructions.
  8. Heat the mixture to a boil over medium-high heat, stirring until dissolved. If using unflavored gulaman, add flavorings like pandan or vanilla and sweeten as needed.
  9. Strain the mixture to filter any undissolved particles.
  10. Let the mixture solidify at room temperature or place it in the refrigerator to speed up the process. Once it's set, release or slice the gulaman into your preferred shapes.
